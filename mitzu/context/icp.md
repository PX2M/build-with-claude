# ICP: who it is for, and who it is not

> Step 1 of the chain. Everything after this (the jobs, the messaging, the brain, the agents)
> is written for the people named here. Get this wrong and every output is aimed at nobody.
> Public facts only, from mitzu.io. Sources at the bottom.

## The reader of every output
A rep, ninety seconds before a demo. Skimmable. Objection, why it lands, what to say back, one source.

## The ICP, in one line
Product people who need answers about how their product is used, at companies that already keep
that data in a warehouse. Plus the data lead who owns that warehouse.

## Who they are
- **Product people.** Product managers, senior PMs, directors and heads of product. On mitzu.io:
  a Senior Product Manager (Transfr) and a Director of Product Management (Colossyan).
- **The data lead who owns the warehouse.** Data leads, data architects, heads of data
  engineering, lead analysts. On mitzu.io: Shapr3D, Suunto, Khatabook, Prezi, 52 Entertainment.
- **Around them:** marketing and leadership who ask the same questions. On mitzu.io: a CMO
  (Munch) and a CTO (Rythm).
- **Company profile:** product and business data already sits in a warehouse (Snowflake,
  BigQuery, Databricks, Redshift, ClickHouse and others), often collected with Segment, Snowplow
  or RudderStack.
- **Trigger:** the bill for a second copy of the events, or the wait for the data team to answer
  a product question.

## Buyer vs user
Two different people. The card has to work for both.

| | The user | The buyer (and the gate) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who | PMs, product leads, marketers, leadership | The data lead who owns the warehouse |
| What they want | Answers today, without writing SQL, without a ticket | No second copy of the data, numbers that match theirs, fewer ad hoc requests |
| What they fear | "I will ask it wrong and not know" | "A new tool in my warehouse, and a new bill" |
| On mitzu.io | "We went from waiting days for reports to getting instant answers." (CMO, Munch) | "Our engineering team no longer gets flooded with ad-hoc SQL requests." (CTO, Rythm) |

Mitzu's pricing page draws the same line. The Analyst plan is "for a small data team". The Team
plan is "for companies where product, marketing and leadership use the data alongside the
analysts." Mitzu does not publish who signs. Our read: the product lead wants it, the data lead
has to say yes, because it is their warehouse.

## The four objections the buyer raises (the battle card covers all four, in these words)
These are how the objections sound on a call. They are the rep's notes, not customer quotes.

1. "Why would I bet on a startup over Amplitude?" (the product lead, and whoever signs)
2. "Moving our data is going to be painful, and IT will have questions." (the product lead, who
   assumes a new tool means a migration)
3. "Our PMs and marketers don't write SQL." (the data lead, on behalf of the users)
4. "Security won't let a new tool into our warehouse." (the data lead)

## Anti-ICP (these do more work than the ICP)
- No warehouse. Disqualify.
- Wants session replay, in-app guides, experiments or feature flags in the same tool. Amplitude
  or Mixpanel fits better. Say so.
- Wants open-ended statistical exploration. That is a notebook, not Mitzu.
